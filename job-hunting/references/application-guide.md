# Application Guide

Practical rules for resumes, cover letters, outreach, and interviews. Read this
before writing any application material.

## Resume / CV

**Structure (reverse-chronological, most common and ATS-safe):**
1. Header — name, one-line title, location, email, LinkedIn/GitHub/portfolio.
2. Short summary (2–3 lines) — only if it adds signal; tailor it to the target.
3. Experience — most recent first, quantified bullets.
4. Skills — grouped (languages, frameworks, tools, cloud).
5. Education, certifications, notable projects.

**Bullet formula — result first:** lead with the outcome, then how.
- Weak: "Responsible for the payments API."
- Strong: "Cut payment failures 32% by redesigning the retry logic in the payments API."
- Pattern: *Accomplished [X] measured by [Y] by doing [Z].*

**Tailoring & ATS:**
- Mirror the exact keywords/skills from the job posting — ATS filters on them.
- Match their phrasing (if they say "CI/CD," don't only write "build pipelines").
- Keep formatting simple: no tables/columns/text-boxes/images that break parsers.
  Standard headings, standard fonts, .docx or PDF as requested.
- Length: 1 page for early career, up to 2 for experienced. Cut anything old or irrelevant.

**Quantify everywhere.** Numbers, %, scale, time saved, revenue, users, latency.
If the user doesn't have exact figures, help them estimate honestly.

## Cover letter

Only when it adds value or is requested. Keep it to ~250–350 words, four beats:
1. **Hook** — why this role/company specifically (show you did homework).
2. **Why them** — what draws you to their mission/product.
3. **Why you** — 2–3 concrete proofs mapped to their needs (reuse resume wins).
4. **Close** — enthusiasm + a light call to action.

Never generic. If it could be sent to any company, rewrite it.

## Outreach & networking messages

Most roles are filled through referrals, so outreach matters.
- Keep it short (3–5 sentences), specific to the person, and easy to say yes to.
- Lead with a genuine reason you're reaching out; make a small, clear ask
  (a quick chat, a pointer, a referral) rather than "can you get me a job."
- Personalize the first line — no copy-paste openers.

## LinkedIn quick wins
- Headline = target identity, not just current title.
- About section mirrors the resume summary, in first person.
- Experience bullets carry the same quantified wins.
- Turn on "open to work"; use keywords recruiters actually search.

## Interview prep
- **STAR** for behavioral answers: Situation, Task, Action, Result — anchor each in a real achievement.
- Prepare stories for common themes: conflict, failure, leadership, ambiguity, impact.
- For technical roles, know the role's core loop (system design, coding, or domain deep-dives) and practice out loud.
- Always prepare 3–4 thoughtful questions to ask them.
- Research the company, the interviewers, and recent news beforehand.

## Salary & offers
- Ground expectations in current market data for the role, level, and location (search for it).
- Let them anchor first when possible; give a researched range, not a single number.
- Negotiate the whole package (base, equity, bonus, sign-on, start date, remote), not just base.

## Market norms vary — adapt, don't assume
The conventions above lean US/Western. Adjust to the user's market before
applying them:
- **Resume vs CV & length** — 1–2 page resume in the US; longer academic-style
  CVs are normal elsewhere.
- **Photo & personal details** — expected on CVs in parts of Europe/Asia/Latin
  America; deliberately omitted in the US/UK/Canada to avoid bias.
- **Job boards** — the dominant board differs by country (e.g. local boards
  often beat global ones); ask or research which matters where they are.
- **Referrals vs cold applications** — some markets are overwhelmingly
  referral-driven; weight networking accordingly.
When unsure of a market's norm, research it rather than defaulting to US rules.

## Honesty
Everything here is about presenting real experience well — never about
inventing it. Don't fabricate metrics or inflate titles; estimate unknown
numbers honestly and label them as estimates. It has to survive an interview
and a reference check.
