# Soft Skills Coach

Improve the soft skills the user's target market actually asks for, and turn
them into evidence a recruiter can see. Every claim traces to a real event —
coached skills still obey the honesty guardrail.

## 1. Assess: demand vs supply

- **Demand** — collect the soft skills named in the user's target postings
  (communication, teamwork, leadership, problem-solving, adaptability,
  stakeholder management, mentoring…). Reuse jd-match extractions when
  available; otherwise skim 3–5 representative postings for the target role.
- **Supply** — the profile's soft-skills entries plus one grouped batch of
  short questions (Step 1.5 style, selectable options), e.g. "Which feels
  hardest: presenting to stakeholders / pushing back on scope / leading
  without authority / handling ambiguity?" and "Describe one recent moment
  where that went badly."

The **gap list** = skills the target roles demand that the profile can't
evidence with a concrete story.

## 2. Plan: small and concrete, 2–3 skills at a time

For each gap skill, produce:
- **Practice actions** — 2–3 low-cost actions inside their current job or
  community: run the next standup, write the incident/postmortem summary,
  demo one feature to non-engineers, mentor one junior, present at a local
  meetup, own one cross-team negotiation.
- **Evidence targets** — where the skill will show once practiced:
  - a result-first resume bullet (quantified where possible),
  - a proof line usable in a cover letter,
  - a STAR story (Situation, Task, Action, Result) for interviews, built
    from the real event the practice action creates.

## 3. Track

Record goals in the profile's "Soft skills development" section: skill, why
it matters (which postings ask for it), actions, status, and the evidence
bank of STAR stories collected. When the section looks stale (untouched for
a few weeks), ask for a one-line progress update — a nudge, not a lecture.
As evidence accumulates, feed it back into the resume, letters, and
interview prep.
